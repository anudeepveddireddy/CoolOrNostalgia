Does 1995's Jumanji still hold up as a genuinely terrifying family classic—or are Robin Williams nostalgia and childhood trauma doing all the heavy lifting?

🧊 Released on December 15, 1995, Joe Johnston's $65 million fantasy-adventure opened at #1—beating both Toy Story and Michael Mann's Heat—and roared to $262.8 million worldwide. Yet today it holds a surprisingly modest 56% Critics / 63% Audience score on Rotten Tomatoes, making it the ultimate test of whether a 90s icon is truly cool or just nostalgia talking.

🔥 In this episode, we dig deep into Jumanji's wild story-arc loopholes—from the 1969 timeline reset that quietly erases Judy and Peter, to Van Pelt's store-bought shotgun bullet getting sucked into an 1869 board game—while celebrating the genius Freudian dual-casting of Jonathan Hyde as both Alan's father and Van Pelt, Robin Williams's vulnerable performance, and ADI's practical animatronics. Plus, ahead of Jumanji: Open World, we pitch our own wild prequels and reboots!
