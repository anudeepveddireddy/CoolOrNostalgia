Is Escape from New York (1981) the ultimate gritty anti-hero classic—or is Snake Plissken the most overrated "Special Forces" operative in 80s cinema?

🧊 Released on July 10, 1981 against juggernauts like Raiders of the Lost Ark and Superman II, John Carpenter's $6 million dystopian thriller grossed $25.2 million domestically and earned an 87% Critics / 77% Audience score on Rotten Tomatoes. Featuring matte paintings and miniature FX from a young James Cameron—and inspiring Hideo Kojima's Metal Gear Solid (which Carpenter personally saved from a lawsuit!)—it transformed former Disney kid Kurt Russell into cinema's ultimate outlaw.

🔥 In this episode, we dive deep into the story arc to unpack the movie's wild logical fallacies—from Snake's surprisingly tame infiltration tactics and the 22-vs-23-hour timer goof to the Duke's mined-bridge escape plan—while giving huge credit to the synth score, $6M practical world-building, and that legendary cassette-swap ending. Plus, we pitch our own modern sequels (including a Wyatt Russell legacy pass!).
